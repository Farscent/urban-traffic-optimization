# Urban Mobility & Traffic Optimization 🚦

**Objective:** Reduce the average rush-hour commute time for metropolitan residents from 60 minutes to 30 minutes within a 5-year timeline.

*Note: This project is adapted from a case simulation completed during the McKinsey Forward Program (Problem Solving Module).*

## 📊 Executive Summary
A rapidly growing metropolitan area ("Hustleton City") faced severe economic and environmental strains due to compounding gridlock. Data analysis revealed a drastic 35% drop in public transit usage over two years, correlating directly with a surge in personal vehicle dependency. 

## 🧠 Methodology: MECE Issue Tree
To break down the core drivers of congestion, I utilized a Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive (MECE) framework to map out potential interventions across public transit improvements, private vehicle disincentivization, and infrastructure optimization.

## 💡 Strategic Recommendations
Using a 2x2 Impact vs. Feasibility matrix, I prioritized two immediate action items to reverse the transit decline and flatten the rush-hour curve:

1. **Supply-Side Optimization:** Redesign and expand the road-based public transit (bus) network to provide full city coverage with simplified routing, directly targeting cost-conscious commuters.
2. **Demand-Side Shifting:** Partner with local corporate stakeholders to implement staggered work hours and hybrid schedules, physically removing a percentage of peak-hour commuter volume without requiring heavy infrastructure investment.
